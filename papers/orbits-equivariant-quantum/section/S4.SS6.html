4.6. Computing $[\mathcal{O}_{M}]$ for a general $d \times n$ matrix

Recall the formulas from Section 2.3. Note that $\int_{\mb{P}^r}[\hbar]f(H)\star g(H)=[z^r][F(z)]\overline{f}(z)\overline{g}(z)=0$ for degree reasons. Applying $\star f_n$ to the right of both sides of Theorem 4.5 [B1EB] and applying $\int_{\mb{P}^r}$, we deduce that $$\int_{(\mb{P}^r)^n}[\mathcal{O}_{M_{d+1}}]f_1(H_1) \cdots f_n(H_n)=[z^r][F(z)^{d}]\overline{f_1}(z)\cdots \overline{f_n}(z).$$ Note if we assume Theorem 4 [48CA], then this also follows from $$[\mathcal{O}_{M_{d+1}}]^{\dagger}=[\mathcal{O}_{\tau^{\le d+1}I_{n}}]^{\dagger}=[z^r][F(z)^{d}][I_{n}]_{\hbar}.$$ We will use this to construct the formula for $[\mathcal{O}_{M_{d+1}}]$.

Theorem 4.7. (EB57)

The equivariant Chow class $[\mathcal{O}_{M_{d+1}}]$ is given by $$\begin{aligned}[\mathcal{O}_{M_{d+1}}]=[z^r][F(z)^{d}]\prod_{i=1}^n \frac{F(z)-F(H_i)}{z-H_i}.\end{aligned}$$

Proof. (B7C5)

For any polynomial $f$ with coefficients in $A^\bullet_{GL_{r+1}}(\pt)$, we have $$\begin{aligned}\int_{\mb{P}^r} \frac{F(z)-F(H)}{z-H}f(H)&=\int_{\mb{P}^r} \frac{F(z)-F(H)}{z-H}\overline{f}(H) \\ &= \int_{\mb{P}^r} \frac{F(z)-F(H)}{z-H} \overline{f}(z) + \int_{\mb{P}^r} \frac{F(z)-F(H)}{z-H}(\overline{f}(H)-\overline{f}(z)) \\ &=\int_{\mb{P}^r} \frac{F(z)-F(H)}{z-H} \overline{f}(z) + \int_{\mb{P}^r} \frac{\overline{f}(z)-\overline{f}(H)}{z-H}(F(H)-F(z)) \\ &= \int_{\mb{P}^r}\frac{F(z)-F(H)}{z-H}\overline{f}(z) + 0 - 0 \\ &= \overline{f}(z);\end{aligned}$$ that is, $\frac{F(z)-F(H)}{z-H}\in A^\bullet_{GL_{r+1}}(\mb{P}^r)\otimes QH^\bullet_{GL_{r+1}}(\mb{P}^r)$ is Kronecker dual to the lifting map $A^\bullet_{GL_{r+1}}(\mb{P}^r)\to QH^\bullet_{GL_{r+1}}(\mb{P}^r)$. Hence $$\int_{(\mb{P}^r)^n}\prod_{i=1}^n \frac{F(z)-F(H_i)}{z-H_i}f_i(H_i)=\prod_{i=1}^n \overline{f_i}(z).$$ Applying $[z^r][F(z)^{d}]$ to both sides of this equation yields the desired result.

Corollary 4.8. (570A)

The expression $$\sum_{|A|=d+1}\left(\prod_{j \not \in A}F(z_j)\right)\sum_{i \in A}\prod_{j \ne i}\frac{(-1)^{n-d+1}}{z_i-z_j}$$ is a polynomial in $z_1,\ldots,z_r$, and $[\mathcal{O}_{M_{d+1}}]$ is obtained by evaluating this polynomial at $z_i=H_i$.

Proof. (2025)

Apply partial fraction decomposition to the denominator of the expression in Theorem 4.7 [EB57]. This will also follow from the general expression in Theorem 9.1 [C75C].
