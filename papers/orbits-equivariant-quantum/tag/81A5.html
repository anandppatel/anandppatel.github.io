Proof of Theorem 9.1 [C75C]. (81A5)

If $\rk(M) < d$, then $\mathcal{O}_{M} = 0$, so we may assume $\rk(M) = d$. First, we will show for all $r$ that substituting $F(H) = H^{r+1}$ into the right-hand side of (3) yields the expression for the non-equivariant class $[\mathcal{O}_{M}] \in A^\bullet((\mb{P}^r)^n)$ with degree at most $r$ in each $H_i$.

Let $\alpha = (r+\frac{1}{n})(1,\ldots,1)$ and let $P'_M = \alpha -(r+1)P_M$. By Theorem 5.2 [48AB], the non-equivariant class $[\mathcal{O}_{M}]$ is the sum of $\prod_{i=1}^n H_i^{e_i}$ over all $(e_i)\in P'_M$, and this sum has degree at most $r$ in each $H_i$. Hence $[\mathcal{O}_{M}] = \phi(P'_M) (H_1,\ldots, H_n)$ non-equivariantly, so we wish to show that $$\phi(P'_M) = \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \left(\prod_{i \in \{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus B(\sigma)} z_i^{r+1}\right)\frac{1}{(z_{\sigma(2)}-z_{\sigma(1)})\cdots (z_{\sigma(n)}-z_{\sigma(n-1)})}.$$ We will do so using the following lemma.

Lemma 9.4. (6836)

Let $P \subset \mb{R}^n$ be a rational convex polytope. Then $$\phi(P) = \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \phi(P + C_\sigma).$$

Proof. (F298)

Let $\mc{P}$ be the convex hull of the vectors $$w_\sigma = -(\sigma^{-1}(1), \ldots, \sigma^{-1}(n)) + ((n+1)/2, \ldots, (n+1)/2)$$ for all $\sigma \in S_n$. (This is the permutohedron [Hoh12, Example 2.6] translated so that it contains $0$.) The tangent cone to $\mc{P}$ at $w_\sigma$ is $w_\sigma + C_\sigma$.

For a sufficiently small rational number $\epsilon > 0$, the polytopes $P$ and $P_\epsilon := P + \epsilon \mc{P}$ have the same lattice points, so $\phi(P) = \phi(P_\epsilon)$. Now, by the description of vertices of Minkowski sums in Section A and Brion's Theorem [BHS09, p.3], we have $\phi(P) = \phi(P_\epsilon)$ can be written as $$\sum_{u \in \Vt(P_\epsilon)} \phi(T_{u, P_\epsilon}) = \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \sum_{v \in \Vt(P)} \phi(T_{v, P} + \epsilon w_\sigma + C_\sigma) = \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \phi(P + \epsilon w_\sigma + C_\sigma).$$ In each equality we used the vanishing of $\phi$ on cones containing lines.

Since $- w_\sigma \in C_\sigma$, the polytopes $P + \epsilon w_\sigma + C_\sigma$ and $P + C_\sigma$ have the same lattice points for sufficiently small $\epsilon$. Hence this sum is equal to $\sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \phi(P + C_\sigma)$ as desired.

Continuing the proof of Theorem 9.1 [C75C], applying Lemma 9.4 [6836] with $P = P'_M$ we obtain $$\begin{aligned}\phi(P'_M) = \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \phi(P'_M + C_\sigma) =\sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \phi(\alpha-(r+1)P_M + C_\sigma) = \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \phi(\alpha-(r+1)(P_M + C_\sigma))\end{aligned}$$ where the last equality comes from replacing $\sigma$ with its reverse $\sigma^r = (\sigma(n), \ldots, \sigma(1))$. By Lemma 9.3 [8355], the term of this sum is equal to

$$\begin{aligned}\phi(\alpha-(r+1)(e_{B(\sigma)} + C_\sigma)) &= \left(\prod_{i \in \{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus B(\sigma)} z_i^{r+1}\right) (z_1 \cdots z_n)^{-1} \phi((1/n,\ldots,1/n) - C_\sigma)\\ &= \left(\prod_{i \in \{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus B(\sigma)} z_i^{r+1}\right)(z_1 \cdots z_n)^{-1} z_{\sigma(1)} \frac{1}{\left(1 - \frac{z_{\sigma(1)}}{z_{\sigma(2)}}\right) \cdots \left(1 - \frac{z_{\sigma(n-1)}}{z_{\sigma(n)}}\right)} \\ &= \left(\prod_{i \in \{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus B(\sigma)} z_i^{r+1}\right)\frac{1}{(z_{\sigma(2)}-z_{\sigma(1)})\cdots (z_{\sigma(n)}-z_{\sigma(n-1)})}\end{aligned}$$ where we used the fact that the polytopes $(1/n,\ldots,1/n) - C_\sigma$ and $e_{\sigma(1)} - C_\sigma$ have the same lattice points, from which (4) follows.

Let $f_M$ be the rational function $$\begin{aligned}f_M(x_1,\ldots,x_n,y_1,\ldots,y_n) &= \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \left(\prod_{i \in \{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus B(\sigma)} y_i\right)\frac{1}{(x_{\sigma(2)}-x_{\sigma(1)})\ldots (x_{\sigma(n)}-x_{\sigma(n-1)})},\end{aligned}$$ and let $Q_{M}(x_1,\ldots,x_n,y_1,\ldots,y_n)$ be the rational function from Theorem 8.2 [367A]. Then, by the above and Theorem 8.2 [367A], we have for all $r$ that the expressions $$\begin{aligned}\text{$f_M(z_1,\ldots,z_n,z_1^{r+1},\ldots,z_{n}^{r+1})$ and $Q_{M}(z_1,\ldots,z_n,z_1^{r+1},\ldots,z_n^{r+1})$}\end{aligned}$$ are both polynomials of degree at most $r$ in each $z_i$ that yield the non-equivariant class $[\mathcal{O}_{M}] \in A^\bullet((\mb{P}^r)^n)$ after substituting $z_i = H_i$. Therefore, they are equal.

Since the set $$\bigcup_{r=0}^\infty \{(z_1,\ldots,z_n,z_1^{r+1},\ldots,z_{n}^{r+1}) \mid z_1, \ldots, z_n \in K\}$$ is Zariski dense in $\mb{A}^{2n}$ (any polynomial of total degree $k$ vanishing on the $k$th term of this union is zero), this implies that $f_M = Q_M$, and the theorem follows from Theorem 8.2 [367A].
