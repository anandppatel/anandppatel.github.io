Proof. (69B4)

Let $W$ be a 3-dimensional vector space. We have a birational isomorphism

Interpret the space $(W^* \otimes U \otimes \Sym^2 V) / \GL(W) \times \GL(U)$ as the space of $2 \times 3$ matrices with entries in $\Sym^2 V$, modulo row and column transformations. Set $S = \Sym^2 V$; it has a canonical (up to scaling) quadratic form given by the conic $Q \cong \P V\subset \P S$ embedded by $\O(2)$. We can then interpret $(W^* \otimes U \otimes \Sym^2 V) / \GL(W) \times \GL(U)$ as the space of $2 \times 3$ matrices with entries in $S$. We have a rational map

It is easy to check that this map is a birational isomorphism—a general triple of points in $\P S$ is the zero locus of $2 \times 2$ minors of a matrix of linear forms, which is uniquely determined up to row and column transformations. By taking a further quotient by $\GL(V)$, we finish the proof.
