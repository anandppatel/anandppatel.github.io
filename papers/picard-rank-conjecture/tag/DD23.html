Proof. (DD23)

Retain the notation introduced above. For brevity, set $U = U_{E^\gen, F^\gen}$ and $U^\dagger = U^\dagger_{E^\gen, F^\gen}$. By Proposition 1.2 [70FF] and Proposition 4.9 [9DD7], we have $$\begin{aligned}\rk \Pic_\Q U \leq \rk \Pic_\Q U^\dagger + \rk\chi(\PGL_2) &= \rk \Pic_\Q U^\dagger\\ &\leq \rk \Pic_\Q V^\circ + \rk\chi(G) \leq 1 + \rk\chi(G).\end{aligned}$$ The final inequality follows because $V^\circ$ is an open subset of a projective space. Let $e$ be the number of divisorial components of $\td \H_{3,g} \setminus U$. We then get the bound $$\rk \Pic_\Q \td \H_{4,g} \leq \rk \Pic_\Q U + e \leq 1 + \rk\chi(G) + e.$$ Recall that $G = G_{F^\gen} \times G_{E^\gen}$.

If $g$ is an odd multiple of $3$, then $$\begin{aligned}G &= \PGL_{2} \times \PGL_{3} \\ \rk\chi(G) &= 0\\ e &= 2 \quad (M\text{ and }CE).\end{aligned}$$ Here $M$ is the component from Proposition 4.5 [CE6B], and $CE$ is the component from Proposition 4.7 [72FD].

If $g$ is odd, but not divisible by $3$, then $$\begin{aligned}G &= \PGL_2 \times G_E \\ G_E &= \left\{\begin{pmatrix} a & b & l_{1}\\ c & d & l_{2}\\ 0 & 0 & e \end{pmatrix} \mid a, b, c, d, e \in \C, e(ad-bc) \in \C^{*}, l_{i} \in H^0(\P^1, O(1)) \right\} \big{/} \C^*. \\ \rk\chi(G) &= 1 \\ e &= 1 \quad (CE).\end{aligned}$$ Here $CE$ is the component from Proposition 4.7 [72FD].

If $g$ is even and divisible by $3$, then $$\begin{aligned}G &= G_F \times \PGL_2 \\ G_F &= \left\{\begin{pmatrix} a & l \\ & b \end{pmatrix} \mid a, b \in \C^*, l \in H^0(\P^1, O(1)) \right\} \big{/} \C^*\\ \rk\chi(G) &= 1\\ e &= 1 \quad (M).\end{aligned}$$ Here $M$ is the component from Proposition 4.5 [CE6B].

If $g$ is even and not divisible by $3$, then $$\begin{aligned}G &= G_F \times G_E \text{ where $G_F$ and $G_E$ are as in the previous two cases,}\\ \rk\chi(G) &= 2 \\ e &= 0.\end{aligned}$$ In all cases, we get $$\rk \Pic_\Q\td \H_{4,g} \leq 3.$$ By Proposition 2.15 [D177], the classes in $\Pic_\Q \td \H_{4,g}$ of the three components of $\td \H_{4,g} \setminus \H_{4,g}$ are linearly independent. Therefore, we get $\Pic_\Q\H_{4,g} = 0$ as desired.
