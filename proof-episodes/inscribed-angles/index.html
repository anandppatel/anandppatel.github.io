The theorem

The inscribed-angle theorem

For three distinct points A, B, C on a circle, angle ABC is half the measure of the arc AC that does not contain B. Consequently, angles subtending the same chord from the same side are equal.

Which arc?

The intercepted arc joins A to C and does not contain B. Its degree measure is the turn at O along that arc; it may exceed 180°. A smaller central angle alone would give the wrong answer when angle ABC is obtuse.

One diameter covers every case

E is the point opposite B on the circle. If E lies inside the intercepted arc, add the two component angles and arcs. If E lies outside it, subtract the smaller from the larger. If E is an endpoint, one component is zero and no degenerate triangle argument is needed.

Moving points and measuring angles

After constructing the circle, A, B, and C move along that fixed circle. Holding A and C still lets you explore how the angle changes as B crosses from one arc to the other. Read the complete proof

This written argument is also available without the interactive diagram. The diagram illustrates the proof; measurements do not establish the result.

  1. Name the arc that the angle sees

    Let A, B, C be distinct points of a circle with center O. Write θ for angle ABC and m for the measure of the arc AC that does not contain B.

    • 0° < θ < 180°
    • 0° < m < 360°

  2. Draw a diameter through the vertex

    Let E be the point opposite B on the circle. The points B, O, E are collinear, with O between B and E. Join O to A and C.

    • OB = OA = OC = OE

  3. A chord and the diameter

    Take X to be either A or C, with X different from E. Triangle BOX is isosceles, so its base angles OBX and BXO are equal. Since BE is the ray BO, denote either angle by αX.

    • ∠OBX = ∠BXO = αX
    • ∠XBE = αX

  4. The exterior angle doubles

    The angle XOE is an exterior angle of triangle BOX, because OE extends OB. It therefore equals 2αX. This is the measure of the arc XE not containing B. If X = E, that arc and angle XBE are both zero, so the same identity holds directly.

    • measure of arc XE avoiding B = 2∠XBE

  5. Add when the diameter is inside

    If E lies on the intercepted arc AC, the ray BE lies inside angle ABC. Add the two component angles at B and the two adjacent arcs to obtain m = 2θ. The same argument permits a zero component when E is A or C.

    • θ = ∠ABE + ∠EBC
    • m = 2∠ABE + 2∠EBC = 2θ

  6. Subtract when the diameter is outside

    If E lies outside the intercepted arc AC, the smaller of arcs AE and CE is contained in the larger, and BE lies outside angle ABC. Subtract their measures and the corresponding angles at B. Again m = 2θ.

    • θ = |∠ABE − ∠CBE|
    • m = |2∠ABE − 2∠CBE| = 2θ

  7. The inscribed-angle theorem

    The sum, difference, and endpoint cases exhaust the possible positions of E. Thus every inscribed angle is half the measure of the arc it intercepts, even when that arc is larger than a semicircle.

    • ∠ABC = ½ measure of arc AC avoiding B

  8. Angles on the same chord

    Two vertices on the same side of chord AC lie on the same arc from A to C. Their inscribed angles therefore intercept the other, identical arc. Both angles are half its measure, so they are equal.

    • Same chord and same side ⇒ equal inscribed angles
