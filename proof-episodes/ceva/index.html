The theorem

Ceva’s theorem

For D, E, and F strictly inside sides BC, CA, and AB, respectively, the segments AD, BE, and CF meet at one point if and only if (BD/DC) × (CE/EA) × (AF/FB) = 1.

Before the proof

Keep D and E fixed and move F along AB: can more than one position make the three segments meet?

What we use

  • Areas and common heights

  • The angle-bisector theorem
What is a cevian?

A cevian joins a vertex of a triangle to a point on the opposite side; three cevians are concurrent when they share a point.

Interior points only

This episode keeps D, E, and F strictly inside their sides, so every ratio and every area used in the proof is positive; extensions require a signed version.

Why the first two meet

The line AD separates B from E, so BE crosses it; the crossing is inside the triangle, hence inside the segment AD.

How to explore

Drag the vertices and the three side points, or focus a point and use the arrow keys; “Make the cevians meet” moves only F.

Read the cyclic order

Follow the boundary B → C → A → B: the three ratios are BD/DC, CE/EA, and AF/FB, in that order.
