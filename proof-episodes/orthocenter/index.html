The observation

Three altitudes, one point.

For every noncollinear triangle, the three altitude lines meet at a unique point H, called the orthocenter.

Why H exists

Draw a parallel through each vertex to make a larger triangle; its perpendicular bisectors turn out to be our original altitudes.

Why A lies between Q and R

In parallelogram ABCQ, AQ points in the direction from B to C. In parallelogram ACBR, AR points from C to B. These directions are opposite, and both lengths equal BC > 0, so Q, A, and R occur in that order.

Why we use altitude lines

For an obtuse triangle, some altitudes meet the extensions of the opposite sides, and H lies outside the triangle. The complete lines always meet, even when the altitude segments do not.

For a right triangle, H is the right-angle vertex: the two legs already lie on altitude lines. The line through each vertex perpendicular to its opposite side remains well defined even when H is that vertex.
