Why O exists

A perpendicular bisector crosses a segment at its midpoint at a right angle.

We will use one fact: it is exactly the set of points equally far from the segment’s two endpoints.

Why the equal-distance fact holds

Let U and V be distinct points, and let M be the midpoint of UV.

If P lies on its perpendicular bisector and P ≠ M, the right triangles PMU and PMV are congruent: they share PM and have MU = MV. Thus PU = PV. If P = M, the equality follows directly from the midpoint definition.

Conversely, suppose PU = PV. If P is on line UV, it must be M. Otherwise, triangles PMU and PMV are congruent by three equal sides. Their angles at M are equal and add to 180°, so each is 90°. In either case P lies on the perpendicular bisector.
