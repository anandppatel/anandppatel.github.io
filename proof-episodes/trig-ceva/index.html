The theorem

Trigonometric Ceva

For D, E, F strictly inside BC, CA, AB, the cevians AD, BE, CF are concurrent if and only if (sin ∠BAD / sin ∠DAC) × (sin ∠CBE / sin ∠EBA) × (sin ∠ACF / sin ∠FCB) = 1.

Sine for an angle

For an acute angle θ, sin θ is opposite leg / hypotenuse in a right triangle; sin 90° = 1, and for 90° < θ < 180° use sin θ = sin(180° − θ). The proof derives the needed area formula from this definition.

Positive angles and ratios

Every side point is strictly interior, so each split angle is between 0° and 180° and its sine is positive; divisions in the proof are therefore valid.

Keep the cyclic order

At A start from AB, at B start from BC, and at C start from CA; the numerator and denominator angles follow that order.

Both directions are proved

The two products are equal for every placement of the three side points; ordinary Ceva then gives both implications without assuming that the cevians already meet.

Free exploration

The proof leaves D, E, and F independently movable; “Make the cevians meet” places F once and does not lock it afterward.
