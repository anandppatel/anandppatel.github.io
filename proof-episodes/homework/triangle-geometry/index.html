Throughout, ABCABC is a noncollinear triangle, and [XYZ][XYZ] denotes the area of triangle XYZXYZ. Write G,O,H,IG,O,H,I for the centroid, circumcenter, orthocenter, and incenter.

You may use The Basics, the results of the episodes linked with each problem, and results established in earlier problems. When asked to prove a statement, give its argument rather than simply citing that statement.

Use the interactive diagrams to investigate. Your written solution should explain why the conclusion holds.

1. An area test for the centroid

From the centroid episode.

(a) Let DD be the midpoint of BCBC. Use AG=2GDAG=2GD, equal bases, and common heights to prove

[GAB]=[GBC]=[GCA].[GAB]=[GBC]=[GCA].

(b) Now let PP be any point strictly inside ABCABC. The ray APAP meets BCBC at an interior point XX, with A,P,XA,P,X in that order. Prove

[PAB][PCA]=BXXC.\frac{[PAB]}{[PCA]}=\frac{BX}{XC}.

Hint: Compare triangles PABPAB and PXBPXB using bases on AXAX, then make the corresponding comparison from CC.

(c) Deduce that

[PAB]=[PBC]=[PCA]⟺P=G.[PAB]=[PBC]=[PCA] \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P=G.

For the reverse direction, explain why equal areas put PP on two medians.

2. When does a median contain the circumcenter?

From the circumcenter and Thales episodes.

Let DD be the midpoint of BCBC.

(a) Suppose AB=ACAB=AC. Use the perpendicular-bisector characterization to prove that OO lies on the line ADAD.

(b) Conversely, suppose OO lies on ADAD and O≠DO\neq D. Prove that AB=ACAB=AC. Explain where the assumption O≠DO\neq D enters.

(c) Show that this assumption cannot be omitted. Consider a triangle with a right angle at AA and unequal legs ABAB and ACAC. Locate its circumcenter and explain why it gives a counterexample.

3. The orthocenter of the midpoint triangle

From the orthocenter and circumcenter episodes; use C3 and the parallel-line facts.

Let D,E,FD,E,F be the midpoints of BC,CA,ABBC,CA,AB, respectively.

(a) Use a dilation centered at AA, with factor 1/21/2, to prove EF∥BCEF\parallel BC. Obtain the other two corresponding parallelisms. Explain why D,E,FD,E,F are distinct and noncollinear.

(b) Show that the altitude line from DD in triangle DEFDEF is precisely the perpendicular bisector of BCBC.

(c) Repeat the argument at EE and FF. Deduce that the circumcenter OO of ABCABC is the orthocenter of DEFDEF.

4. Finding the inradius by adding areas

From the incenter episode and C4.

a=BC,a=BC,b=CA,b=CA,c=AB,c=AB,s=a+b+c2.s=\frac{a+b+c}{2}.

(a) Draw the perpendiculars from II to the three sides. Express [IAB][IAB], [IBC][IBC], and [ICA][ICA] in terms of a,b,c,ra,b,c,r.

(b) Explain why these three triangles partition ABCABC, and prove

(c) Suppose BC=14BC=14, CA=15CA=15, AB=13AB=13, and the altitude from AA to BCBC has length 1212. Find the inradius and the areas [IAB][IAB], [IBC][IBC], and [ICA][ICA].

5. Four points on the Euler line

From the Euler-line episode.

Suppose ABCABC is not equilateral, and let NN be the midpoint of OHOH.

(a) The episode constructs XX on the ray opposite GO→\overrightarrow{GO}, with GX=2GOGX=2GO, and uses similar triangles to prove X=HX=H. Explain why GG lies between OO and HH, with GH=2GOGH=2GO.

(b) Determine the order of O,G,N,HO,G,N,H along their common line. Prove

GN=12GO,GN=\frac12GO,HN=3GN.HN=3GN.

(c) Describe how to construct HH and NN when the distinct points OO and GG are already marked.

(d) What changes when ABCABC is equilateral? Explain why the four centers then determine no unique line.

6. Does a 2:12:1 ratio identify the centroid?

From the angle-bisector, incenter, and centroid episodes.

Let the internal angle bisector from AA meet BCBC at DD.

(a) Explain why II lies strictly between AA and DD. Using common heights, prove

[ABI][DBI]=AIID,\frac{[ABI]}{[DBI]}=\frac{AI}{ID},[ACI][DCI]=AIID.\frac{[ACI]}{[DCI]}=\frac{AI}{ID}.

(b) Add the corresponding area equations from part (a). Then use the equal perpendicular distances from II to the three side lines to prove

AIID=AB+ACBC.\frac{AI}{ID}=\frac{AB+AC}{BC}.

(c) Suppose AB=5AB=5, AC=7AC=7, and BC=6BC=6. Find BDBD, DCDC, and AI:IDAI:ID. Prove that I≠GI\neq G, despite the ratio you obtain.

Explain what is missing from the claim: “A point dividing a segment from a vertex in the ratio 2:12:1 must be the centroid.”

7. Constructing an altitude foot with two circles

From Thales’ theorem and its converse and C1.

Draw the circles with diameters ABAB and ACAC. Let DD be the perpendicular foot from AA to the full line BCBC.

(a) First suppose D≠B,CD\neq B,C. Prove that DD lies on both circles.

(b) Prove the same conclusion when D=BD=B or D=CD=C. Identify the right angle of ABCABC in each case.

(c) Suppose P≠AP\neq A lies on both circles. Prove P=DP=D. Handle P=BP=B or P=CP=C separately; otherwise, use Thales’ theorem and uniqueness of a perpendicular through PP.

Conclude that the circles meet at exactly AA and DD, including when the altitude foot lies outside segment BCBC.

8. A second construction of the midpoint circle

Inspired by the Euler-line relation and the dilation in the nine-point-circle proof.

Let D,E,FD,E,F be the side midpoints, RR the circumradius, and NN the midpoint of OHOH. Consider the transformation TT consisting of a half-turn about GG, followed by a dilation of factor 1/21/2 about GG.

Use the centroid theorem, Problem 5, and C2–C3; do not assume the nine-point-circle theorem in this problem.

(a) Prove

T(A)=D,T(A)=D,T(B)=E,T(B)=E,T(C)=F.T(C)=F.

(b) Prove T(O)=NT(O)=N. Explain separately why this holds when ABCABC is equilateral.

(c) Deduce that the circumcircle of DEFDEF has center NN and radius R/2R/2. Also determine [DEF]/[ABC][DEF]/[ABC].

9. Reflecting the orthocenter onto the circumcircle

An extension of nine-point-circle Part I and Part II.

Let A1A_1 be the altitude foot from AA on the full line BCBC, and let H′H' be the reflection of HH across that line. Prove that H′H' lies on the circumcircle of ABCABC.

(a) If H≠A1H\neq A_1, prove that A1A_1 is the midpoint of HH′HH'. What is H′H' when H=A1H=A_1?

(b) Let SS be the dilation centered at HH with factor 22. Recall that the nine-point circle has center NN, the midpoint of HOHO, and radius R/2R/2. Prove that SS sends this circle onto the circumcircle.

(c) Part II places A1A_1 on the nine-point circle. Identify S(A1)S(A_1) and finish the proof. Explain why the argument remains valid when H=A1H=A_1.

10. Ceva’s theorem and the areas around the intersection

From the Ceva episode.

Let DD and EE lie strictly inside BCBC and CACA, respectively, with

(a) Determine the ratio AF/FBAF/FB for which an interior point FF of ABAB makes AD,BE,CFAD,BE,CF concurrent. Explain why there is exactly one such FF.

(b) Let PP be their common point, and put

x=[PAB],x=[PAB],y=[PBC],y=[PBC],z=[PCA].z=[PCA].

Use the common-height comparisons from the proof of Ceva to determine x:y:zx:y:z. Identify the triangles compared at each step.

(c) If [ABC]=54[ABC]=54, find each of the three areas x,y,zx,y,z.
