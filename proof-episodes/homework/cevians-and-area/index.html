Throughout, ABCABC is a noncollinear triangle. Write a=BCa=BC, b=CAb=CA, c=ABc=AB, s=(a+b+c)/2s=(a+b+c)/2, and Δ=[ABC]\Delta=[ABC]. All side points in the Ceva problems lie strictly inside their sides. Angles are ordinary interior angles, and all lengths and areas are positive.

You may use The Basics, ordinary Ceva, the episodes linked below, and earlier problems in this set. When a problem asks you to establish a result from an episode, reconstruct its argument rather than citing the result itself. The sine-area formula and the identity sin⁡(180∘−θ)=sin⁡θ\sin(180^\circ-\theta)=\sin\theta are explained in Episode 11.

Write KK for the symmedian point, NaN_a for the Nagel point, and GeG_e for the Gergonne point. Here NaN_a names the Nagel point; it is not the nine-point center NN. Write II for the incenter, rr for the inradius, and IA,rAI_A,r_A for the center and radius of the excircle opposite AA.

Try each part before opening its hints. Each hint suggests a first step and a further comparison if needed. The Markdown download and printed set include all hints and figures. Drawings show sample configurations; your argument must cover every triangle allowed by the hypotheses.

1. Turn a side ratio into an angle ratio

From trigonometric Ceva.

Let DD lie strictly inside BCBC.

(a) Use a common height to express [ABD]/[ACD][ABD]/[ACD] in terms of BDBD and DCDC.

(b) Compute those same two areas using 12uvsin⁡θ\tfrac12uv\sin\theta. Prove

BDDC=ABACsin⁡∠BADsin⁡∠DAC.\frac{BD}{DC}=\frac{AB}{AC}\frac{\sin\angle BAD}{\sin\angle DAC}.

Explain why the sine-area formula still applies if one of the displayed angles is obtuse.

(c) Write the corresponding identities for E∈CAE\in CA and F∈ABF\in AB, in the cyclic orders CE/EACE/EA and AF/FBAF/FB.

(d) Multiply the identities and use ordinary Ceva to prove both directions of trigonometric Ceva. Explain why every denominator is nonzero.

Hints for Problem 1
  • (a) Use bases BDBD and DCDC on the same line. Their common perpendicular height cancels in the ratio, even if its foot is outside the side.
  • (b) Use sides AB,ADAB,AD for the first area and AC,ADAC,AD for the second. Cancel AD/2AD/2; for an obtuse angle, its supplementary acute angle gives the same perpendicular height and sine.
  • (c) Replace A,B,C,DA,B,C,D successively by B,C,A,EB,C,A,E and C,A,B,FC,A,B,F. Keep the angle next to the numerator side in the numerator sine.
  • (d) Group the three side-length factors before cancelling. The resulting equality identifies the entire sine product with the ordinary Ceva product, so the equivalence follows in both directions; each split angle lies strictly between 0∘0^\circ and 180∘180^\circ.

2. Reflect a concurrent family of cevians

From trigonometric Ceva, preparing for the symmedian episode.

Suppose AD,BE,CFAD,BE,CF meet at an interior point PP. Reflect each cevian ray across the internal angle bisector at its vertex; let the reflected rays meet the opposite sides at D′,E′,F′D',E',F'.

(a) Explain why the reflected rays still lie strictly inside their vertex angles and therefore meet the opposite sides at interior points. Identify the two angles at AA that reflection exchanges.

(b) Show that the sine ratio for AD′AD' is the reciprocal of the sine ratio for ADAD, and obtain the two cyclic statements.

(c) Use trigonometric Ceva to show that AD′,BE′,CF′AD',BE',CF' meet at a unique interior point QQ.

(d) Repeat this construction starting with QQ. Prove that the resulting point is PP. Explain why II stays fixed under this construction.

Hints for Problem 2
  • (a) Reflection interchanges the two sides of the vertex angle and preserves angles between rays. A ray strictly between the sides crosses the opposite side of the triangle; use the convexity of the triangle.
  • (b) Write the two exchanged angles before taking their sines. The numerator for the original ray becomes the denominator for its reflection.
  • (c) Multiply the three reciprocal ratios. A reciprocal of a product equal to 11 is again 11; interior Ceva supplies concurrence, and the first two cevians determine its point.
  • (d) A reflection performed twice is the identity. Apply this separately at all three vertices; for II, each ray is itself an internal angle bisector and hence stays fixed under the corresponding reflection.

3. Recognize a symmedian from a squared ratio

From the symmedian episode.

Let MM be the midpoint of BCBC, and let ADAD be the reflection of the median ray AMAM across the internal angle bisector at AA.

(a) Apply Problem 1 to AMAM to find sin⁡∠BAM/sin⁡∠MAC\sin\angle BAM/\sin\angle MAC in terms of AB,ACAB,AC.

(b) Reflect the two angles and apply Problem 1 again to prove

BDDC=(ABAC)2.\frac{BD}{DC}=\left(\frac{AB}{AC}\right)^2.

(c) Prove the converse: an interior cevian satisfying this squared ratio is the symmedian. Use uniqueness of a point dividing a fixed segment in a prescribed positive ratio.

(d) Suppose AB=5AB=5, AC=7AC=7, and BC=6BC=6. Find BDBD and DCDC, and compare DD with the median foot and the angle-bisector foot. Give their order from BB to CC.

Hints for Problem 3
  • (a) The left side of Problem 1's identity is BM/MC=1BM/MC=1. Solve for the sine ratio, paying attention to which side length moves to the other side.
  • (b) Reflection interchanges the two angles, so it reciprocates the sine ratio from part (a). The side-length factor in Problem 1 remains AB/ACAB/AC.
  • (c) The reflected median already supplies one point with the required ratio. If BD/DC=kBD/DC=k, use BD+DC=BCBD+DC=BC to show that BDBD, and hence DD, is determined uniquely.
  • (d) Use the ratios 25:4925:49, 1:11:1, and 5:75:7 for the symmedian, median, and bisector. Convert each ratio into its fraction of the total length measured from BB before comparing positions.

4. Areas and distances around the symmedian point

From the symmedian episode.

Let the three symmedians meet at KK. Put U=[KBC]U=[KBC], V=[KCA]V=[KCA], and W=[KAB]W=[KAB].

(a) If AKAK meets BCBC at DD, prove W/V=BD/DCW/V=BD/DC by comparing areas with common heights. You may use Problem 1(b) of the first homework set, with its justification.

(b) Use the three squared side ratios to prove

U:V:W=a2:b2:c2.U:V:W=a^2:b^2:c^2.

(c) Let ha,hb,hch_a,h_b,h_c be the perpendicular distances from KK to the lines BC,CA,ABBC,CA,AB. Deduce ha:hb:hc=a:b:ch_a:h_b:h_c=a:b:c.

(d) Prove that K=IK=I if and only if ABCABC is equilateral. Explain both implications.

Hints for Problem 4
  • (a) First compare [KAB][KAB] with [KDB][KDB], using bases AK,KDAK,KD; repeat from CC. Then compare [KDB][KDB] with [KDC][KDC], whose bases lie on BCBC.
  • (b) Part (a) and Problem 3 give one ratio between WW and VV. Repeat cyclically, then check the order of U,V,WU,V,W against the opposite sides a,b,ca,b,c.
  • (c) Write U=aha/2U=ah_a/2, and write the two corresponding formulas. Dividing the area ratios by the base-length ratios removes one power of each side length.
  • (d) If K=IK=I, its three distances to the sides are equal, so use part (c). Conversely, in an equilateral triangle every median is also an angle bisector and is fixed by reflection in that bisector.

5. Where an excircle touches

From the Nagel episode.

The excircle opposite AA touches BCBC at TT, the ray ABAB beyond BB at VV, and the ray ACAC beyond CC at UU. You may use the episode's construction of this excircle and the stated positions of its contact points.

(a) Use equal tangent lengths from one point to show AV=AUAV=AU, BV=BTBV=BT, and CU=CTCU=CT.

(b) Put AV=AU=ℓAV=AU=\ell. Express BTBT and CTCT in terms of ℓ,b,c\ell,b,c, and use BT+CT=aBT+CT=a to prove ℓ=s\ell=s.

(c) Deduce BT=s−cBT=s-c and CT=s−bCT=s-b. Explain how these differ from the corresponding incircle contact lengths s−bs-b and s−cs-c.

(d) If a=13,b=14,c=15a=13,b=14,c=15, find BT,CT,BV,CU,AVBT,CT,BV,CU,AV. Explain why the triangle inequalities ensure that both pieces of BCBC are positive for every noncollinear triangle.

Hints for Problem 5
  • (a) Group the tangent segments by their common external point: first AA, then BB, then CC. A contact with a side extension still lies on the same tangent line.
  • (b) The orders are A,B,VA,B,V and A,C,UA,C,U. Subtract AB=cAB=c from AVAV, and AC=bAC=b from AUAU; then add the two lengths on BCBC.
  • (c) Substitute the value of ℓ\ell from part (b). For the incircle, write the three vertex tangent lengths and solve the three equations expressing a,b,ca,b,c as their pairwise sums.
  • (d) Calculate ss first, and reuse parts (a)–(c). For positivity, write s−b=(a+c−b)/2s-b=(a+c-b)/2 and s−c=(a+b−c)/2s-c=(a+b-c)/2.

6. Build the Nagel point with Ceva

From the Nagel episode.

Let TA,TB,TCT_A,T_B,T_C be the contacts of the three excircles with their respective opposite sides BC,CA,ABBC,CA,AB.

(a) Use Problem 5 cyclically to express BTA/TACBT_A/T_AC, CTB/TBACT_B/T_BA, and ATC/TCBAT_C/T_CB in terms of s−a,s−b,s−cs-a,s-b,s-c.

(b) Multiply the ratios and prove that ATA,BTB,CTCAT_A,BT_B,CT_C have a unique common point NaN_a inside ABCABC.

(c) For a=13,b=14,c=15a=13,b=14,c=15, find the three ratios in part (a). Check their product without using rounded decimals.

(d) Prove that ATAAT_A is a median exactly when AB=ACAB=AC. In an isosceles triangle with AB=ACAB=AC, explain why NaN_a lies on its symmetry axis.

Hints for Problem 6
  • (a) Keep the boundary order B→C→A→BB\to C\to A\to B. Write the numerator and denominator tangent lengths separately before making each fraction.
  • (b) Each of the three positive quantities should occur once above and once below the fraction bar. Apply the interior version of Ceva, and use the intersection of the first two cevians for uniqueness.
  • (c) List s−a,s−b,s−cs-a,s-b,s-c first. Keep fractions exact so that the cancellation in their product remains visible.
  • (d) A median foot has equal distances to BB and CC. Substitute the two lengths from Problem 5; in the isosceles case, the median from AA is the symmetry axis and contains the common point.

7. Build the Gergonne point with Ceva

From the Gergonne episode.

Let the incircle touch BC,CA,ABBC,CA,AB at D,E,FD,E,F, respectively, and write x=AF=AEx=AF=AE, y=BD=BFy=BD=BF, and z=CD=CEz=CD=CE.

(a) Write the three side lengths in terms of x,y,zx,y,z. Solve for x,y,zx,y,z in terms of s,a,b,cs,a,b,c.

(b) Express BD/DCBD/DC, CE/EACE/EA, and AF/FBAF/FB in terms of x,y,zx,y,z. Prove that AD,BE,CFAD,BE,CF meet at a unique interior point GeG_e.

(c) Suppose the tangent lengths from A,B,CA,B,C are 4,3,24,3,2. Find the side lengths and the three Ceva ratios, and check the product.

(d) Is IGeIG_e necessarily a nonzero segment? Analyze an equilateral triangle and justify the position of GeG_e.

Hints for Problem 7
  • (a) Each side is the sum of two tangent lengths. Add the three equations to express ss, then subtract one side at a time.
  • (b) Follow the boundary order from the preceding problem. Each of x,y,zx,y,z appears once in a numerator and once in a denominator, so interior Ceva applies.
  • (c) Use the sums from part (a), with the letters attached to their vertices. The three fractions should cancel before you perform any decimal division.
  • (d) Equal side lengths force equal vertex tangent lengths. Locate the contact points, identify their cevians, and compare their concurrence point with the incenter.

8. Compare the two contact constructions

From the Nagel and Gergonne episodes.

Use D,E,FD,E,F for the incircle contacts and TA,TB,TCT_A,T_B,T_C for the opposite excircle contacts.

(a) Prove that DD and TAT_A are reflections of one another across the midpoint of BCBC. Obtain the two cyclic statements.

(b) Deduce that the three Nagel side ratios are the reciprocals of the three Gergonne side ratios, in the same cyclic order. Explain directly why one product is 11 exactly when the other is 11.

(c) Suppose Na=GeN_a=G_e. Explain why D=TAD=T_A, then deduce b=cb=c. Repeat at another vertex to prove that ABCABC is equilateral.

(d) Prove the converse: in an equilateral triangle, Na=Ge=G=IN_a=G_e=G=I. State precisely why the shared point is the centroid.

Hints for Problem 8
  • (a) Compare BD,DCBD,DC with BTA,TACBT_A,T_AC using Problems 5 and 7. Reflection in the midpoint reverses the two distances to the segment's endpoints.
  • (b) Reversing numerator and denominator reciprocates a positive ratio. Multiply the three reciprocals together before invoking any concurrence theorem.
  • (c) The common center is interior and hence different from AA; one line through AA and that center meets BCBC in only one point. Equal feet have both the incircle and excircle contact-length descriptions.
  • (d) All six contacts are side midpoints in this case. The three contact cevians are therefore medians, and an equilateral triangle's medians are also its internal angle bisectors.

9. Reconstruct Venema’s proof of Heron’s formula

From the Heron episode, following Venema, §4.5, Figure 4.4 on printed p. 44 and Exercises 4.5.1–4.5.7 on printed p. 45 (PDF viewer pp. 61–62). The questions below are an adaptation of that proof sequence.

The incircle touches BC,CA,ABBC,CA,AB at X,Y,ZX,Y,Z; the excircle opposite AA touches the corresponding sidelines at T,U,VT,U,V, with UU beyond CC and VV beyond BB. Write E=IAE=I_A for the excenter in this problem, as in Venema’s figure.

(a) By adding the areas of IAB,IBC,ICAIAB,IBC,ICA, prove Δ=sr\Delta=sr. Use equal tangent lengths to obtain AZ=s−aAZ=s-a, BZ=s−bBZ=s-b, BV=s−cBV=s-c, and AV=sAV=s.

(b) Prove that right triangles AZIAZI and AVEAVE are similar, with the correspondence A↔AA\leftrightarrow A, Z↔VZ\leftrightarrow V, I↔EI\leftrightarrow E. Deduce

rAs=rs−a.\frac{r_A}{s}=\frac{r}{s-a}.

(c) Explain why the internal bisector BIBI and external bisector BEBE are perpendicular. Prove △ZBI∼△VEB\triangle ZBI\sim\triangle VEB, in the displayed vertex order, and deduce

s−br=rAs−c.\frac{s-b}{r}=\frac{r_A}{s-c}.

(d) Eliminate rAr_A from parts (b)–(c), use Δ=sr\Delta=sr, and prove

Δ=s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c).\Delta=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}.

Explain why the positive square root is the one required.

Hints for Problem 9
  • (a) The three smaller triangles have height rr to their respective side bases. Reuse the tangent-length calculations from Problems 5 and 7; remember that AV=AB+BVAV=AB+BV, since VV lies beyond BB.
  • (b) The radii to contact points are perpendicular to the tangent line ABAB. Both AIAI and AEAE are the internal angle-bisector ray at AA; compare the side opposite that angle with the side along ABAB in each triangle.
  • (c) At BB, the internal and external angles sum to 180∘180^\circ, so their halves sum to 90∘90^\circ. Identify the angle at EE in the second right triangle that equals the angle at BB in the first, then compare BZ/ZIBZ/ZI with EV/VBEV/VB.
  • (d) Rewrite part (b) as an expression for rAr_A, and rewrite part (c) as an expression for rrAr r_A. Substitute to find r2r^2, then multiply by s2s^2. Every factor s−a,s−b,s−cs-a,s-b,s-c and the area itself is positive.

10. What Heron’s formula tells us

From the Heron episode and Problem 9.

(a) For a triangle with a=13,b=20,c=21a=13,b=20,c=21, compute ss and its area using Heron’s formula. Show the factorization under the square root.

(b) Find its inradius and the radius of its excircle opposite the side of length 1313. Derive the needed excircle-area relation from Problem 9(b) and Δ=sr\Delta=sr.

(c) All three side lengths are multiplied by a positive number tt. Use Heron’s formula to show how area changes, then determine how the two radii in part (b) change.

(d) Rewrite Heron’s identity as

16Δ2=(a+b+c)(−a+b+c)×(a−b+c)(a+b−c).\begin{aligned} 16\Delta^2={}&(a+b+c)(-a+b+c)\\ &{}\times(a-b+c)(a+b-c). \end{aligned}

Explain what happens to this expression as a triangle with fixed positive b,cb,c flattens with a→b+ca\to b+c. Distinguish a limiting area of zero from the noncollinear hypothesis of the theorem.

Hints for Problem 10
  • (a) Compute s−a,s−b,s−cs-a,s-b,s-c before multiplying. Group the four positive factors into perfect squares, or factor them into primes.
  • (b) The inradius follows from Δ=sr\Delta=sr. In Problem 9(b), multiply by s(s−a)s(s-a) and substitute sr=Δsr=\Delta before solving for rAr_A.
  • (c) The semiperimeter and each difference s−a,s−b,s−cs-a,s-b,s-c acquire one factor of tt. Count the factors before taking the square root; then use the area-radius relations from part (b).
  • (d) Replace each of the four factors involving ss by its expression with denominator 22. In the limit, identify the factor that tends to zero; the formula is applied along noncollinear triangles before taking that limit.
