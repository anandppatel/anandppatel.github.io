These problems use the proof episodes to investigate new configurations. The episode results are available tools: explain how they apply to the problem at hand. The questions include testing a proposed rule, finding a new construction, recovering a triangle from geometric data, and proving an area bound.

Throughout, ABCABC is noncollinear. Write a=BCa=BC, b=CAb=CA, c=ABc=AB, s=(a+b+c)/2s=(a+b+c)/2, and Δ=[ABC]\Delta=[ABC]. All side points used in Ceva's theorem lie strictly inside their sides, and its ratios use ordinary positive lengths and sines. Write G,I,K,Na,GeG,I,K,N_a,G_e for the centroid, incenter, symmedian point, Nagel point, and Gergonne point. The symbol NaN_a denotes the Nagel point, not the nine-point center.

You may use The Basics, ordinary Ceva, the linked episodes, and earlier problems in this set. For reconstruction, you may also use the elementary construction fact that three positive numbers are the sides of a noncollinear triangle exactly when each is smaller than the sum of the other two. SSS gives uniqueness up to congruence once the corresponding side lengths are fixed.

In Problems 9–10, rr is the inradius and rA,rB,rCr_A,r_B,r_C are the labeled exradii opposite A,B,CA,B,C. The Heron episode supplies

Δ2=s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c),\Delta^2=s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c),Δ=sr,\Delta=sr,Δ=(s−a)rA=(s−b)rB=(s−c)rC.\Delta=(s-a)r_A=(s-b)r_B=(s-c)r_C.

Try each part before opening its hints. The Markdown download and printed set include all hints and figures. Drawings illustrate sample configurations; a proof must cover every triangle allowed by the stated hypotheses.

1. When angle ratios give the wrong prediction

An application of trigonometric Ceva.

Let ABCABC be a right isosceles triangle with ∠A=90∘\angle A=90^\circ and AB=AC=1AB=AC=1. Choose interior side points D∈BCD\in BC, E∈CAE\in CA, and F∈ABF\in AB so that

∠BAD=60∘,∠DAC=30∘,\angle BAD=60^\circ,\quad \angle DAC=30^\circ,∠CBE=15∘,∠EBA=30∘,\angle CBE=15^\circ,\quad \angle EBA=30^\circ,

and CFCF is the internal angle bisector at CC. You may use the exact value sin⁡15∘=(6−2)/4\sin15^\circ=(\sqrt6-\sqrt2)/4.

(a) Find the two angles at CC cut out by CFCF, and compute the product

∠BAD∠DAC∠CBE∠EBA∠ACF∠FCB,\frac{\angle BAD}{\angle DAC} \frac{\angle CBE}{\angle EBA} \frac{\angle ACF}{\angle FCB},

where all six angles are measured in degrees. What would this product suggest if one mistakenly replaced the sine ratios in trigonometric Ceva by ratios of angles?

(b) Compute the correct product of sine ratios exactly. Prove that AD,BE,CFAD,BE,CF are not concurrent; a decimal approximation alone is not a proof.

(c) Keep DD and EE fixed. Find BD/DCBD/DC and CE/EACE/EA, and determine the ratio AF∗/F∗BAF_*/F_*B required for AD,BE,CF∗AD,BE,CF_* to be concurrent.

(d) Prove that there is exactly one such point F∗F_* in the interior of ABAB. Find AF∗/ABAF_*/AB, and decide whether F∗F_* lies between AA and FF or between FF and BB.

Hints for Problem 1
  • (a) First find the two acute angles of the right isosceles triangle. The bisector divides the angle at CC into equal parts. Keep the ordinary angle product separate from the sine product.
  • (b) Substitute the standard values of sin⁡30∘\sin30^\circ and sin⁡60∘\sin60^\circ, together with the supplied value of sin⁡15∘\sin15^\circ. After simplifying, you can test equality with 11 by squaring an equality between positive quantities.
  • (c) Use the side–sine identity from Episode 11 at AA and then at BB; the side-length factor at BB is not 11. Once two side ratios are known, ordinary Ceva determines the third.
  • (d) A positive ratio AF∗/F∗B=kAF_*/F_*B=k, together with AF∗+F∗B=ABAF_*+F_*B=AB, determines one point of the segment. Find the corresponding ratio for the original angle-bisector foot FF, and compare the two positions using exact inequalities between positive numbers.

2. A family of concurrent cevians

An application of trigonometric Ceva and the symmedian episode.

For each integer n≥0n\geq0, choose interior side points Dn∈BCD_n\in BC, En∈CAE_n\in CA, and Fn∈ABF_n\in AB with

BDnDnC=(cb)n,\frac{BD_n}{D_nC}=\left(\frac cb\right)^n,CEnEnA=(ac)n,\frac{CE_n}{E_nA}=\left(\frac ac\right)^n,AFnFnB=(ba)n.\frac{AF_n}{F_nB}=\left(\frac ba\right)^n.

Here a=BCa=BC, b=CAb=CA, and c=ABc=AB.

(a) Explain why each of these three points exists and is unique. Express BDn/BCBD_n/BC in terms of b,c,nb,c,n, and write the two cyclic formulas.

(b) Use the side–sine identity from Episode 11 to compute the three sine ratios for ADn,BEn,CFnAD_n,BE_n,CF_n. Apply trigonometric Ceva to prove that they meet at a unique interior point PnP_n.

(c) Identify P0,P1,P2P_0,P_1,P_2 among the familiar triangle centers. For a triangle with a=5,b=6,c=7a=5,b=6,c=7, find the exact fraction BD3/BCBD_3/BC.

(d) Suppose Pm=PnP_m=P_n for two distinct nonnegative integers m,nm,n. Prove that the triangle must be equilateral. Deduce that every nonequilateral triangle has infinitely many distinct points in this family, and describe what happens for an equilateral triangle.

Hints for Problem 2
  • (a) For a segment of length LL and a prescribed ratio x/(L−x)=k>0x/(L-x)=k>0, solve for xx. Check that the result is strictly between 00 and LL.
  • (b) The side–sine identity has one extra side-length factor. Divide by it before multiplying the three sine ratios. The case n=0n=0 involves reciprocals, which are defined because all side lengths are positive.
  • (c) Compare the ratios obtained at n=0,1,2n=0,1,2 with the characterizations of a median, an internal angle bisector, and a symmedian. For n=3n=3, convert the ratio of two lengths into a fraction of their sum.
  • (d) An interior point fixes a single ray from AA, hence a single intersection with BCBC. If two members of the family coincide, compare their two ratios on BCBC, and then do the same at another vertex. Separate positive numbers less than 11, equal to 11, and greater than 11 when comparing unequal integer powers.

3. The symmedian point of a right triangle

A geometric application of the symmedian episode.

Suppose ∠A=90∘\angle A=90^\circ. Let DD be the foot of the perpendicular from AA to BCBC, and let LL be the midpoint of ADAD.

(a) Explain why DD lies in the interior of BCBC. Use similar triangles to prove

BD=AB2BC,BD=\frac{AB^2}{BC},DC=AC2BC.DC=\frac{AC^2}{BC}.

(b) Prove that ADAD is the symmedian from AA. Then use area comparisons to show

[LBC]=[ABC]2,[LBC]=\frac{[ABC]}2,[LAB]=[ABD]2.[LAB]=\frac{[ABD]}2.

(c) Let the ray BLBL meet ACAC at EE. Compare areas to find CE/EACE/EA, and prove that BLBL is the symmedian from BB. Deduce that K=LK=L. If AB=3AB=3 and AC=4AC=4, find AKAK exactly.

(d) Now allow ABCABC to be any noncollinear triangle whose altitude foot DD lies strictly inside BCBC. Prove that ADAD is a symmedian if and only if either AB=ACAB=AC or ∠A=90∘\angle A=90^\circ.

Hints for Problem 3
  • (a) The angles at BB and CC are acute. In the two smaller right triangles, identify which acute angle also occurs in ABCABC, and keep corresponding sides in the same order.
  • (b) Use the squared-side criterion for a symmedian. For the area equalities, one comparison uses base BCBC and the other uses bases AL,ADAL,AD on the same line.
  • (c) Compare [LBC][LBC] with [EBC][EBC] using bases on BEBE, and make the same comparison with [LAB][LAB] and [EAB][EAB]. The common factor cancels. Then compare the latter two triangles using bases on ACAC. For the numerical value, compute the same total area using the legs and using the altitude to the hypotenuse.
  • (d) Write x=BDx=BD, y=DCy=DC, and h=ADh=AD. Express AB2AB^2 and AC2AC^2 by Pythagoras, substitute into the symmedian criterion, and factor the resulting equality. Interpret each factor geometrically; use the converse of Pythagoras when needed.

4. Constructing a symmedian with tangents

An application of the circumcenter and symmedian episodes.

Let ABCABC have circumcircle Γ\Gamma, center OO, and radius RR. Assume the tangents to Γ\Gamma at BB and CC meet at a finite point TT, and that the segment ATAT crosses the interior of BCBC at DD. These position assumptions are part of the configuration.

(a) Prove the following elementary fact. If X,YX,Y are distinct points on a circle of radius RR, and θ\theta is the angle between the chord ray YXYX and either tangent ray at YY, then

sin⁡θ=XY2R.\sin\theta=\frac{XY}{2R}.

Use the midpoint of XYXY and the radii to prove the formula. Address both tangent directions and the case in which XYXY is a diameter; do not quote the tangent–chord angle theorem.

(b) Use part (a), equal tangent lengths from TT, and the sine-area formula to compute [ABT]/[ACT][ABT]/[ACT] in terms of ABAB and ACAC.

(c) Compare these same areas with [BDT][BDT] and [CDT][CDT]. Prove that ADAD is the symmedian from AA.

(d) Suppose AB=5AB=5, AC=7AC=7, and BC=8BC=8, with the tangent configuration just described. Find BDBD and DCDC exactly, without computing the position of TT. Explain why these lengths determine the crossing point uniquely.

Hints for Problem 4
  • (a) If the chord is not a diameter, its midpoint MM gives a right triangle with hypotenuse RR and one leg XY/2XY/2. The tangent is perpendicular to the radius at YY. Reversing its direction replaces the angle by its supplement. If the chord is a diameter, read the tangent angle directly.
  • (b) Use AB,BTAB,BT for one sine-area formula and AC,CTAC,CT for the other. The angles at the contact points are exactly the angles treated in part (a).
  • (c) The bases ATAT and DTDT lie on one line. Compare triangles with common third vertex BB, then with common third vertex CC, and cancel the common factor. A final common-height comparison uses bases on BCBC.
  • (d) The two pieces have known sum BCBC and a ratio supplied by part (c). Solve these two relations using exact fractions; neither tangent length is needed.

5. The cevian that balances the perimeter

An application of the Nagel point.

Let TAT_A be the contact of the AA-excircle with BCBC. You may use BTA=s−cBT_A=s-c and TAC=s−bT_AC=s-b. A boundary path from AA to a point of BCBC follows the sides through either BB or CC; its length does not include the cevian joining its endpoints.

(a) Show that the two boundary paths A ⁣→B ⁣→TAA\!\to B\!\to T_A and A ⁣→C ⁣→TAA\!\to C\!\to T_A have the same length, and identify that length.

(b) Let DD be any point strictly inside BCBC. Prove that the triangles ABDABD and ACDACD have equal perimeters if and only if D=TAD=T_A. Explain why this gives a unique perimeter-balancing cevian from AA.

(c) Suppose (a,b,c)=(16,14,10)(a,b,c)=(16,14,10). Find BTABT_A, TACT_AC, and the common boundary-path length. Express the common perimeter of the two triangles in terms of ATAAT_A, and find [ABTA]:[ACTA][ABT_A]:[ACT_A]. Does balancing the perimeters also balance the areas in this example?

(d) At each of A,B,CA,B,C, draw the unique cevian that splits ABCABC into two triangles of equal perimeter. Use the Nagel episode to prove that these three cevians have a unique common point. State this as a new geometric characterization of NaN_a, without repeating a Ceva proof.

Hints for Problem 5
  • (a) The path through BB has length c+BTAc+BT_A; use the corresponding expression through CC.
  • (b) Both small-triangle perimeters contain ADAD, so cancel it. If BD=tBD=t, substitute DC=a−tDC=a-t; a prescribed distance from BB determines at most one point of BCBC.
  • (c) Keep the boundary path separate from the closed perimeter: the latter also includes ATAAT_A. For the area ratio, use the common height from AA to BCBC.
  • (d) Apply part (b) at each vertex. Identify the three resulting side points before invoking the known Nagel concurrence theorem.

6. A line through three centers

An application of the Nagel point, incenter, and centroid.

Work in any Cartesian coordinates. In expressions such as uA+vBuA+vB, multiply and add the two coordinates separately. You may use the segment-division identity P=(1−t)A+tDP=(1-t)A+tD when P∈ADP\in AD and t=AP/ADt=AP/AD; no general theorem about area coordinates is assumed.

(a) Put U=[NaBC]U=[N_aBC], V=[NaCA]V=[N_aCA], and W=[NaAB]W=[N_aAB]. Compare areas with common heights and use the known excircle contact ratios to prove

U:V:W=(s−a):(s−b):(s−c).U:V:W=(s-a):(s-b):(s-c).

Determine U,V,WU,V,W individually in terms of Δ\Delta and ss.

(b) Let PP be any interior point, and put UP=[PBC]U_P=[PBC], VP=[PCA]V_P=[PCA], and WP=[PAB]W_P=[PAB]. If the ray APAP meets BCBC at DD, first establish

BDDC=WPVP,\frac{BD}{DC}=\frac{W_P}{V_P},APAD=VP+WPΔ.\frac{AP}{AD}=\frac{V_P+W_P}{\Delta}.

Use segment division to deduce the coordinate identity

P=UPA+VPB+WPCΔ.P=\frac{U_PA+V_PB+W_PC}{\Delta}.

(c) Apply part (b) to II, using its equal perpendicular distances from the sides, and to NaN_a, using part (a). Use the centroid's 2:12:1 median ratio to write coordinates for GG. Combine these expressions to prove

Na=3G−2I.N_a=3G-2I.

(d) When I≠GI\ne G, interpret this identity geometrically: determine the order of I,G,NaI,G,N_a on their line and the ratio GNa:GIGN_a:GI. Prove that I=GI=G occurs exactly for an equilateral triangle, and explain what the identity says in that case.

Hints for Problem 6
  • (a) If ANaAN_a meets BCBC at TAT_A, compare [NaAB]/[NaCA][N_aAB]/[N_aCA] with BTA/TACBT_A/T_AC. Repeat cyclically and then use U+V+W=ΔU+V+W=\Delta.
  • (b) Compare the areas with bases AP,PDAP,PD using heights from BB and CC. Also compare [PBD][PBD] with [ABD][ABD] and [PCD][PCD] with [ACD][ACD], then add. Finally express DD as a weighted point of BCBC and substitute into P=(1−t)A+tDP=(1-t)A+tD.
  • (c) The three triangles with vertex II have the same height, so their areas are proportional to a,b,ca,b,c. For NaN_a, expand each coefficient s−a,s−b,s−cs-a,s-b,s-c and collect the terms involving A+B+CA+B+C.
  • (d) Subtract GG from the identity before interpreting the direction and length. To analyze I=GI=G, compare the three subtriangle areas at II with those at the centroid; in the converse direction use SSS to compare an equilateral triangle's medians and angle bisectors.

7. Recover a triangle from two contact ratios

An inverse problem using the Gergonne construction.

The incircle touches BC,CA,ABBC,CA,AB at D,E,FD,E,F. Suppose only the two positive numbers

u=BDDC,u=\frac{BD}{DC},v=CEEAv=\frac{CE}{EA}

are specified. Write x=AE=AFx=AE=AF, y=BD=BFy=BD=BF, and z=CD=CEz=CD=CE. The episode gives the side equations a=y+za=y+z, b=z+xb=z+x, c=x+yc=x+y.

(a) Determine x:y:zx:y:z from u,vu,v. Hence express a:b:ca:b:c in terms of u,vu,v, keeping the labels of the three sides fixed.

(b) Prove that every pair u,v>0u,v>0 is realized by a noncollinear triangle and that the triangle is unique up to similarity. Check all three strict triangle inequalities, and verify that the incircle of the constructed triangle has the prescribed ratios; merely producing three positive side lengths is not enough.

(c) Recover the side lengths when

u=32,u=\frac32,v=43,v=\frac43,and the perimeter is 52.\text{and the perimeter is }52.

Also find the remaining contact ratio AF/FBAF/FB.

(d) Describe exactly which pairs (u,v)(u,v) produce AB=ACAB=AC, which produce BA=BCBA=BC, and which produce CA=CBCA=CB. Which pair gives an equilateral triangle?

Hints for Problem 7
  • (a) Choose a positive value for xx, use v=z/xv=z/x to find zz, and then use u=y/zu=y/z to find yy. Form the three side sums only afterward.
  • (b) If a=y+za=y+z, b=z+xb=z+x, c=x+yc=x+y, simplify b+c−ab+c-a, c+a−bc+a-b, and a+b−ca+b-c. In the resulting triangle, recover its actual tangent lengths from its semiperimeter. For uniqueness, first match scales by a dilation and then apply SSS.
  • (c) Find a simple integer triple proportional to x,y,zx,y,z. Its three side sums have a definite perimeter, which determines the remaining scale factor.
  • (d) Express each equality of two side sums as an equality of two of x,y,zx,y,z, then translate it into the parameters u,vu,v.

8. How large is the contact triangle?

An area application of the Gergonne construction.

Let D,E,FD,E,F be the incircle contacts on BC,CA,ABBC,CA,AB, and put x=AE=AFx=AE=AF, y=BD=BFy=BD=BF, z=CD=CEz=CD=CE. Thus a=y+za=y+z, b=z+xb=z+x, c=x+yc=x+y, with x,y,z>0x,y,z>0. The triangle DEFDEF is the contact triangle. You may use the sine-area formula from trigonometric Ceva.

(a) Compare each corner triangle with ABCABC using their shared vertex angle. Prove

[AEF]Δ=x2bc,\frac{[AEF]}{\Delta}=\frac{x^2}{bc},[BFD]Δ=y2ca,\frac{[BFD]}{\Delta}=\frac{y^2}{ca},[CDE]Δ=z2ab.\frac{[CDE]}{\Delta}=\frac{z^2}{ab}.

Explain why the argument covers an obtuse triangle.

(b) Subtract the three corner areas from Δ\Delta and prove

[DEF]Δ=2xyzabc.\frac{[DEF]}{\Delta}=\frac{2xyz}{abc}.

For the algebra, multiply by abcabc, replace a,b,ca,b,c by their contact-length sums, and collect terms.

(c) Starting only with (p−q)2≥0(p-q)^2\ge0 for positive numbers p,qp,q, prove

(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)≥8xyz.(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)\ge8xyz.

Determine precisely when equality holds; do not cite an inequality without proving its needed case.

(d) Deduce the sharp bound [DEF]≤Δ/4[DEF]\le\Delta/4, and characterize all triangles attaining it. Describe the contact points in the equality case and give a geometric explanation of the area fraction.

Hints for Problem 8
  • (a) At AA, the numerator uses the two lengths x,xx,x, while the denominator uses b,cb,c; both use sin⁡∠A\sin\angle A. Treat BB and CC in the same cyclic order.
  • (b) The numerator after taking a common denominator is abc−ax2−by2−cz2abc-ax^2-by^2-cz^2. Expand the side sums and look for cancellation of the terms containing a square.
  • (c) Rewrite the squared-difference inequality as a comparison between (p+q)2(p+q)^2 and 4pq4pq. Apply it to all three pairs, multiply, and use positivity when taking a square root.
  • (d) Combine parts (b) and (c), and track the equality conditions through both. If the three tangent lengths are equal, locate each contact on its side and reconsider the three corner triangles from part (a).

9. The largest area with a fixed base and perimeter

An application of Heron’s formula.

Fix numbers a>0a>0 and s>as>a. Consider all triangles whose base BCBC has length aa and whose perimeter is 2s2s; the other two sides may vary.

(a) Put d=b−cd=b-c. Express bb and cc in terms of a,s,da,s,d, and prove that the admissible values of dd are exactly −a<d<a-a<d<a. Check both positivity and all three strict triangle inequalities in the converse direction.

(b) Show that

(s−b)+(s−c)=a,(s-b)+(s-c)=a,(s−b)−(s−c)=−d.(s-b)-(s-c)=-d.

Use a difference of squares and Heron’s formula to deduce

Δ2=s(s−a)4(a2−d2).\Delta^2=\frac{s(s-a)}4\bigl(a^2-d^2\bigr).

(c) Determine the largest possible area and characterize the triangles that attain it. Explain why the maximizing side lengths really occur in a noncollinear triangle and determine that triangle up to congruence.

(d) Suppose a positive number qq is prescribed as the area. Determine exactly when such a triangle exists, and recover b,cb,c, up to interchange, from a,s,qa,s,q. Explain what happens when qq equals the maximum and why q=0q=0 is excluded.

Hints for Problem 9
  • (a) Solve the two equations b+c=2s−ab+c=2s-a and b−c=db-c=d. For a triangle, compare b−cb-c and c−bc-b with aa; conversely, use s>as>a together with the two bounds on dd.
  • (b) For any numbers u,vu,v, the identity 4uv=(u+v)2−(u−v)24uv=(u+v)^2-(u-v)^2 expresses their product using their sum and difference. Apply it to u=s−bu=s-b, v=s−cv=s-c.
  • (c) The factor s(s−a)/4s(s-a)/4 is fixed and positive. Decide which admissible value of dd makes the remaining factor largest, then use part (a) to check that equality is attainable.
  • (d) Substitute Δ=q\Delta=q in part (b) and solve for d2d^2. A real square root is necessary, but also check the strict bound from part (a); the two possible signs of dd exchange bb and cc.

10. Reconstruct a triangle from its circle radii

An inverse problem using Heron’s formula and the tangent lengths from the Gergonne and Nagel episodes.

Four positive numbers are proposed as the inradius rr and the labeled exradii rA,rB,rCr_A,r_B,r_C of a triangle. We will determine exactly when this proposal is possible and whether it determines the triangle.

(a) For any triangle, use the area-radius identities in the setup to prove

1r=1rA+1rB+1rC.\frac1r=\frac1{r_A}+\frac1{r_B}+\frac1{r_C}.

Apply this necessary test to the proposed quadruples

(r,rA,rB,rC)=(1,2,3,6)and(1,2,3,5).(r,r_A,r_B,r_C)=(1,2,3,6) \quad\text{and}\quad (1,2,3,5).

Which is ruled out, and which remains possible? Explain why passing a necessary test alone has not yet proved existence.

(b) Now suppose the four positive numbers satisfy the identity in part (a). Define

Q=r rArBrC,Q=\sqrt{r\,r_A r_B r_C},x=QrA,y=QrB,z=QrC,x=\frac Q{r_A},\quad y=\frac Q{r_B},\quad z=\frac Q{r_C},

and propose side lengths

a=y+z,a=y+z,b=z+x,b=z+x,c=x+y.c=x+y.

Prove that these lengths form a noncollinear triangle. If SS is its semiperimeter, show that S=x+y+z=Q/rS=x+y+z=Q/r and identify S−a,S−b,S−cS-a,S-b,S-c. Explain how x,y,zx,y,z describe its incircle tangent lengths from the vertices.

(c) Use Heron’s formula to prove that the constructed triangle has area QQ. Then use the area-radius identities to show that its inradius and three labeled exradii are exactly the four proposed numbers. Conclude that the condition in part (a) is sufficient as well as necessary.

(d) Prove that any triangle with the proposed radii must have the side lengths constructed in part (b), so the triangle is unique up to congruence with corresponding vertex labels. Reconstruct the candidate from part (a) that passes the test: find its area and three sides, and identify the resulting kind of triangle.

Hints for Problem 10
  • (a) Divide each of s,s−a,s−b,s−cs,s-a,s-b,s-c by the area using the given identities. Add the three expressions involving exradii and use a+b+c=2sa+b+c=2s; keep the candidate tests as exact fractions.
  • (b) Positivity of x,y,zx,y,z should make each difference “sum of two sides minus the third” visibly positive. Add the three proposed sides to find SS, and multiply the reciprocal identity by QQ; compare S−a,S−b,S−cS-a,S-b,S-c with the vertex tangent lengths.
  • (c) Heron’s product for the constructed triangle is SxyzSxyz. Substitute the definitions of S,x,y,zS,x,y,z, then simplify using Q2=r rArBrCQ^2=r\,r_A r_B r_C; both the area and QQ are positive.
  • (d) For any triangle with those radii and area DD, express s,s−a,s−b,s−cs,s-a,s-b,s-c as DD divided by the appropriate radius. Substitute into Heron’s formula to determine D2D^2, then recover the tangent lengths and sides; use SSS for uniqueness and the converse of Pythagoras to identify the concrete triangle.
