The theorem

Ptolemy’s theorem

If A, B, C, and D are distinct vertices of a convex cyclic quadrilateral in that order, then AC · BD = AB · CD + AD · BC.

Before the proof

Convexity is a hypothesis

The vertices are distinct and occur in boundary order A, B, C, D. The two diagonals lie inside the quadrilateral. The drawing places D on the open arc CA that does not contain B; it does not impose Ptolemy’s metric identity.

Why the auxiliary ray meets AC

The diagonal BD lies strictly inside angle ABC. Hence 0 < angle DBC < angle ABC. Copying that angle from BA toward BC gives a ray strictly inside angle ABC, which crosses the opposite side AC at a unique interior point X.

A constructed point, not a solved formula

The diagram obtains X by rotating the direction BA through angle DBC and intersecting the resulting ray with AC. It does not position X by assuming either product identity. Decimal measurements illustrate the proof.

Read the complete proof

This written argument is also available without the interactive diagram. The diagram illustrates the proof; measurements do not establish the result.

  1. Four vertices on one circle

    Let ABCD be a convex cyclic quadrilateral, with four distinct vertices in boundary order.

    • ABCD is convex and cyclic.

  2. An angle inside an angle

    Since BD is an interior diagonal, angle DBC is strictly smaller than angle ABC.

    • 0° < ∠DBC < ∠ABC

  3. Construct X

    Inside angle ABC, copy angle DBC from BA toward BC. The resulting ray meets AC at an interior point X, and angle ABX equals angle DBC.

    • ∠ABX = ∠DBC
    • A, X, C occur in that order.

  4. The first similarity

    Angles BAX and BDC subtend the same chord BC, so they are equal. Together with the copied angles this gives triangle ABX similar to triangle DBC.

    • ∠BAX = ∠BDC
    • △ABX ∼ △DBC

  5. The first side product

    Corresponding sides give AX/CD = AB/BD. Multiplying by CD · BD identifies the first part of the diagonal product.

    • AX · BD = AB · CD

  6. The second similarity

    Subtracting the copied angles from angle ABC gives angle XBC = angle ABD. Also angle XCB = angle ADB, since both subtend chord AB. Hence triangle XBC is similar to triangle ABD.

    • ∠XBC = ∠ABD
    • ∠XCB = ∠ADB
    • △XBC ∼ △ABD

  7. The second side product

    This similarity gives XC/AD = BC/BD, and therefore the second part of the diagonal product is AD · BC.

    • XC · BD = AD · BC

  8. Add the two identities

    Because X lies inside AC, we have AX + XC = AC. Add the two identities and factor out BD.

    • (AX + XC) · BD = AB · CD + AD · BC

  9. Ptolemy’s theorem

    Thus the product of the diagonals equals the sum of the products of opposite sides.

    • AC · BD = AB · CD + AD · BC
