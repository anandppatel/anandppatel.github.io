The theorem

Power of a point

For a circle of center O and radius R, the signed product along any secant through P is OP² − R². For exterior P this is also the squared tangent length PT²; ordinary intersecting-chord products inside the circle equal R² − OP².

Signed power and positive lengths

The power is OP² − R², negative inside the circle, zero on it, and positive outside. If P lies between X and Y, the ordinary distances PX and PY are positive and their product is R² − OP². An exterior secant gives the positive product OP² − R². The signed product uses both endpoint coordinates on the same oriented line.

The sign convention on a secant

Orient the line from X toward Y and put its origin at F, the chord midpoint. Write X = −ℓ, Y = ℓ and P = q in this one-dimensional coordinate. The directed displacements from P are −ℓ − q and ℓ − q. Their product is q² − ℓ². Reversing the orientation reverses both displacements and leaves their product unchanged.

A proper secant is required

X and Y are distinct. For an exterior point the direction control stays strictly between the tangent directions, so the line always meets the circle twice. P itself is free. Tangency is treated separately with a perpendicular radius; we do not infer it by taking an unproved limiting case.

Collapsed right triangles

When the secant passes through O, its perpendicular foot F equals O and the chord is a diameter. When P = F, the length PF is zero. The squared-length identities still hold directly in these cases, so the proof does not require a nondegenerate right triangle at every step.

Read the complete proof

This written argument is also available without the interactive diagram. The diagram illustrates the proof; measurements do not establish the result.

  1. Define the power

    Let the circle have center O and radius R. For any point P, define its power to be OP² − R².

    • Pow(P) = OP² − R²

  2. Choose any proper secant

    Take a line through P meeting the circle at distinct points X and Y. Drop the perpendicular from O to the line, with foot F.

    • OX = OY = R
    • OF is perpendicular to line XY.

  3. The foot bisects the chord

    If O differs from F, the right triangles OFX and OFY are congruent by hypotenuse-leg, so FX = FY. If O = F, the two lengths are already equal radii. Thus F is the midpoint of XY.

    • FX = FY = ℓ > 0

  4. Compare two squared distances

    Orient the line from X toward Y and let q be the signed coordinate of P measured from F. Pythagoras gives OP² = q² + OF² and R² = ℓ² + OF², also when one perpendicular triangle collapses.

    • OP² = q² + OF²
    • R² = ℓ² + OF²
    • OP² − R² = q² − ℓ²

  5. Factor the signed product

    The directed displacements from P to X and Y are −ℓ − q and ℓ − q. Their product is q² − ℓ², and therefore equals the power.

    • (−ℓ − q)(ℓ − q) = q² − ℓ²
    • Directed PX · PY = OP² − R²

  6. Read ordinary positive lengths

    For exterior P, the two directed displacements have the same sign. For interior P, they have opposite signs. If P is on the circle, one displacement is zero.

    • Outside: PX · PY = OP² − R²
    • Inside: PX · PY = R² − OP²
    • On the circle: PX · PY = 0

  7. Compare any two secants

    The expression OP² − R² does not depend on the chosen line. Hence any two secants through the same P have equal signed products, and also equal ordinary products.

    • PX · PY = PW · PZ for ordinary lengths on two secants through P.

  8. Now take an exterior tangent

    Suppose P is exterior and PT is tangent at T. The radius OT is perpendicular to PT, so triangle OTP is right.

    • OT ⟂ PT
    • OT = R

  9. The tangent has the same power

    Pythagoras gives PT² = OP² − OT² = OP² − R². Thus the squared tangent length equals the ordinary product on every secant through exterior P.

    • PT² = OP² − R²
    • PT² = PX · PY

  10. One invariant, three familiar forms

    The intersecting-chord, two-secant, and tangent-secant identities all express this same power, with the interior sign handled separately.

    • Pow(P) = OP² − R²
    • Inside, ordinary chord products equal −Pow(P).
    • Outside, ordinary secant products and tangent squares equal Pow(P).
