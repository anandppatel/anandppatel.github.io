Why the excenter exists in the required region

Let L be the internal A-bisector’s intersection with BC, and put α = ∠A, β = ∠B, γ = ∠C. Choose the exterior B-bisector ray inside the angle between BC and the ray opposite BA. This ray and the ray AL continued past L lie on the far side of BC. Their angles with BL and LB are (180° − β)/2 and α/2 + β, whose sum is 180° − γ/2 < 180°. By P2 their supporting lines cannot be parallel, so they intersect. An intersection on the A-side of BC would give a triangle whose base angles are the supplements of these two angles and sum to more than 180°, contradicting T2. Thus the intersection is on the two chosen rays beyond BC; call it I_a. The two distinct lines give a unique intersection. This constructs the excenter from two bisectors; no circle has been assumed.

Why the contacts are on the stated rays

The equal-distance fact from the incenter episode applies to every angle smaller than 180°, including the exterior angles here. I_a lies inside angle A and beyond BC, so it lies inside the exterior angle at C between CB and the ray opposite CA. Equal distances to CA and CB put it on that angle’s internal bisector. The perpendicular feet from a point on an internal bisector lie on both forward boundary rays. Applying this at B and C places D on both rays BC and CB, hence strictly inside BC; it places V beyond B on AB and U beyond C on AC. None of these contacts can be a vertex.

Why this is the A-excircle

The common perpendicular distance from I_a to the three side lines is positive. The circle with this radius is tangent at D, U, and V by O1. Its disk lies on the far side of BC and in the inward half-planes of AB and AC, since each line is exactly one radius from the center. Thus it lies outside ABC, opposite A. Any circle in that region tangent to all three lines has its center on the same two angle-bisector rays, so this excircle is unique. Repeat cyclically for the other two excircles.

Names and ordinary lengths

Write a = BC, b = CA, c = AB and s = (a + b + c)/2. D, E, F are the contacts on BC, CA, AB of the A-, B-, C-excircles respectively. Na names the Nagel point; N remains the nine-point center. Every ratio in Ceva uses positive segment lengths, because D, E, F are interior side points.

Drawing precision

Nearly flat triangles may have very distant excenters and very large excircles. Fit construction includes each displayed circle in full; Fit triangle returns attention to ABC. Close point labels use ≈, which makes no claim of exact equality.
