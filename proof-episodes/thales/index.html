The theorem

Thales’ theorem

For noncollinear A, B, and C, the angle ACB is a right angle if and only if C lies on the circle with diameter AB.

Before the proof

Drag C inside and outside the circle: when does angle ACB become a right angle?

Build the diagram first, then follow the proof one sentence at a time.

What we use

  • Rigid motions and half-turns
  • Isosceles triangles and SAS
  • The triangle angle sum
  • Parallel lines and angles
Two directions

First start with C on the circle and prove a right angle; then start with a right angle and prove C lies on the circle.

The endpoints are excluded

C must differ from A and B, and the triangle must be noncollinear; otherwise angle ACB is not the angle in this theorem.

What the measurement tells us

Rounded angle measurements suggest the result; the isosceles triangles and the congruence argument prove it.
